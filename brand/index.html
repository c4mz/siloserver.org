02 / usage

Most uses need no permission

If you are writing about Silo, building something that works with it, or running it yourself, use the name and artwork freely. The only thing we ask is that your own project keeps its own identity, so nobody mistakes it for an official Silo release.

Go ahead

  • Describe your work as “compatible with Silo,” “a Silo client,” “a plugin for Silo,” or “based on Silo.”
  • Show the logo beside your product, at a referential size, to signal that the two work together.
  • Write articles, tutorials, reviews, and talks about Silo, with the artwork.
  • Redistribute official, unmodified builds under the Silo name.
  • Run, modify, and self-host Silo for yourself or your organization.

Ask us first

  • Shipping a modified build under the Silo name or logo.
  • Publishing an app to any app store as Silo, or with a look-alike identity.
  • Putting Silo in your company, product, domain, or app name in a way that reads as official. Wordplay is exempt; see naming below.
  • Merchandise, or using the logo or wordmark to sell your own product beyond a “works with Silo” placement.
  • Anything that suggests we run, sponsor, or endorse your offering.

We say yes to most reasonable requests. The policy exists to prevent confusion, not to stop people building on Silo. Never use the name or logo to promote piracy or to impersonate the project.

Naming your project

Pick a name that is yours, then mention Silo in the tagline. That is how the official products are told apart: the server is Silo Server, and official apps join “Silo” to a product word with no space, such as SiloRemote and SiloMac. So that pattern, and “Silo” on its own, are reserved. The test is simple: would a reasonable user think the Silo team made it?

Works well

  • “Bramble — a Silo client for Android TV”
  • “Lantern, a lightweight Silo web player”
  • “Trakt sync plugin for Silo”
  • Repository and package names such as silo-plugin-trakt, where “silo” only describes what it plugs into.
  • Wordplay such as “Siloette” or “Silodeon,” which reads as its own word rather than an official product. This is the exception to the reserved patterns, wherever you publish, as long as you use your own logo.

Please avoid

  • “Silo” alone, or as the first word of a product name, including “Silo Client” or “Silo Player” as the name itself.
  • “Silo[word]” names such as SiloTV, SiloPlay, or SiloFlix, which read as official apps.
  • “[word]Silo” names such as OpenSilo or MySilo, which read as the project itself.
  • Misspellings and look-alikes such as Sylo or Si1o.

Handling the artwork

Do

  • Use the supplied artwork without redrawing it.
  • Leave comfortable clear space on every side.
  • Use the wordmark variant with clear background contrast.
  • Keep your own product name and identity more prominent.

Don’t

  • Stretch, skew, rotate, crop, or rearrange the artwork.
  • Recolor the mark or add shadows, outlines, or effects.
  • Build the mark into your own logo.
  • Use the icon as the identity of a fork or third-party app.

03 / construction

Clear space and size

The mark is built on one rule: every slanted edge sits at exactly 30° from horizontal. You never need to redraw it, but these are the measurements that keep a placement correct.

Clear space

Keep at least the width of one bar counter—54 units, about 20% of the mark's width—free on all four sides. Nothing else sits inside it.

Minimum size

The mark reads cleanly down to about 24 px tall. Below that use the flat cut, and below 16 px use a single element rather than the full stack.

Typography

The wordmark is Plus Jakarta Sans ExtraBold at −0.04 em tracking, converted to outlines. It is artwork, not live text—do not re-typeset it. The website's own faces are not part of the logo.

06 / permissions

Open source code. Protected identity.

The code is AGPL and yours to fork. The name and logo identify the official project, so a distributed fork picks its own name and logo and says it is “based on Silo.” This page is the practical guide; the trademark policy on GitHub is the canonical text. Not sure whether your use is fine? Ask in Discord or email us. We answer quickly and usually say yes.
